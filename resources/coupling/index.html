Mode coupling

Dipole modes in subgiants and red giants are mixtures of an acoustic (p) mode in the envelope and a buoyancy (g) mode in the core. Press Play to take a star from subgiant-like, with several p modes per g mode, to red-giant-like, with many g modes per p mode, and change the coupling strength q to see how strongly the modes repel.

How it works

Pure modes
Pure p modes are evenly spaced in frequency and pure g modes evenly spaced in period:
νp = (n + εp) Δν,    1/νg = (ng + εg) ΔΠ1,   ng = 1, 2, 3, …
The g-mode order starts at ng = 1, so the ng = 1 mode is the highest-frequency g mode and there are no g modes above it. As the star evolves, a shrinking Δν lowers the p modes, while a shrinking ΔΠ1, as the core contracts, raises the g modes, so the two families cross. What sets the character of the spectrum is the number of g modes per Δν, 𝒩 = Δν / (ν2 ΔΠ1): below one, several p modes share each g mode, as in subgiants; well above one, many g modes crowd around each p mode, as in red giants.
The evolution shown
Between t = 0 and 1, Δν falls from 22 to 18 μHz while ΔΠ1 falls from about 5200 to 16 s: slowly at first, quickly through the middle, and slowly again at the end. At t = 0 the ng = 1 g mode sits just below the window, as in an early subgiant, and every mode above it is a p mode; it then rises through the p modes, bumping each in turn, and more g modes follow. At the middle of the window 𝒩 grows to 20, as for a red giant well up the branch; since 𝒩 ∝ ν−2, it is larger at the bottom of the window. This is illustrative: in a real star 𝒩 grows mostly because the oscillations move to lower frequency as the star expands, and ΔΠ1 stays above about 50 s. Here ΔΠ1 shrinks much further instead, so the whole story fits in one frequency window.
Coupling
Mixed modes satisfy the asymptotic relation
tan[π(ν/Δν − εp)] · tan[π(εg − 1/(ν ΔΠ1))] = q
where q is the coupling strength. Above the ng = 1 g mode the g phase is held at −π/2, halfway to the nonexistent ng = 0 mode, so the second factor is infinite and the modes there are pure p modes. Where a p mode and a g mode would cross, the two mixed modes instead repel in an avoided crossing and exchange character; the larger q, the wider the gap. As q → 0 the mixed modes return to the pure p and g modes. The page solves for every mixed mode with Newton's method, following each one as the star evolves.
Échelle diagrams
In the frequency échelle the pure p modes line up at ν mod Δν = εp Δν; in the period échelle the pure g modes line up at P mod ΔΠ1 = εg ΔΠ1 (dashed lines). Mixed modes bend away from both. Their colour, and in the échelles their size, shows how p-like they are: ζ is the fraction of the mode's inertia in the g cavity, so orange, large markers are p-like (ζ near 0), blue, small ones are g-like (ζ near 1), and modes that are half p and half g (ζ = 0.5) are drawn in the neutral middle of the colour scale, dark on a light background and light on a dark one.
