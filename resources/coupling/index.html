Mode coupling

Dipole modes in subgiants and red giants are mixtures of an acoustic (p) mode in the envelope and a buoyancy (g) mode in the core. Press Play to take a star from subgiant-like, with several p modes per g mode, to red-giant-like, with many g modes per p mode, and change the coupling strength q to see how strongly the modes repel.

How it works

Pure modes
Pure p modes are evenly spaced in frequency and pure g modes evenly spaced in period:
νp = (n + εp) Δν,    1/νg = (m + εg) ΔΠ1
As the star evolves, a shrinking Δν lowers the p modes, while a shrinking ΔΠ1, as the core contracts, raises the g modes, so the two families cross. What sets the character of the spectrum is the number of g modes per Δν, 𝒩 = Δν / (ν2 ΔΠ1): below one, several p modes share each g mode, as in subgiants; well above one, many g modes crowd around each p mode, as in red giants.
The evolution shown
Between t = 0 and 1, Δν falls from 22 to 18 μHz while ΔΠ1 falls from about 2600 to 90 s: slowly at first, quickly through the middle, and slowly again at the end. At the middle of the window, 𝒩 grows from 0.15, a single g mode spread over several p modes as in an early subgiant, to 3.5, as on the red-giant branch; since 𝒩 ∝ ν−2, it is larger at the bottom of the window. This is illustrative: in a real star 𝒩 grows mostly because the oscillations move to lower frequency as the star expands. Here ΔΠ1 shrinks much faster instead, so the whole story fits in one frequency window.
Coupling
Mixed modes satisfy the asymptotic relation
tan[π(ν/Δν − εp)] · tan[π(εg − 1/(ν ΔΠ1))] = q
where q is the coupling strength. Where a p mode and a g mode would cross, the two mixed modes instead repel in an avoided crossing and exchange character; the larger q, the wider the gap. As q → 0 the mixed modes return to the pure p and g modes. The page solves for every mixed mode with Newton's method, following each one as the star evolves.
Échelle diagrams
In the frequency échelle the pure p modes line up at ν mod Δν = εp Δν; in the period échelle the pure g modes line up at P mod ΔΠ1 = εg ΔΠ1 (dashed lines). Mixed modes bend away from both. Their colour, and in the échelles their size, shows how p-like they are: ζ is the fraction of the mode's inertia in the g cavity, so orange, large markers are p-like (ζ near 0) and blue, small ones are g-like (ζ near 1).
