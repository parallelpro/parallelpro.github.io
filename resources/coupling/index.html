Mode coupling

Dipole modes in subgiants and red giants are mixtures of an acoustic (p) mode in the envelope and a buoyancy (g) mode in the core. Set how the large separation Δν and the period spacing ΔΠ1 change with time, and watch the pure p and g modes cross while the coupled mixed modes bend away from each other in avoided crossings.

Drag the time slider or press Play, or click the left panel to pick a time. Hover the échelle diagrams for mode details.

How it works

Pure modes
Pure p modes are evenly spaced in frequency and pure g modes evenly spaced in period:
νp = (n + εp) Δν,    1/νg = (m + εg) ΔΠ1
Δν and ΔΠ1 change with time t between the values you set at t = 0 and t = 1, geometrically: Δν(t) = Δν(0) [Δν(1)/Δν(0)]t, and likewise for ΔΠ1. A shrinking Δν lowers the p modes, while a shrinking ΔΠ1, as the core contracts, raises the g modes, so the two families cross.
Coupling
Mixed modes satisfy the asymptotic relation
tan[π(ν/Δν − εp)] · tan[π(εg − 1/(ν ΔΠ1))] = q
where q is the coupling strength. The page rewrites it as a smooth phase that increases steadily with frequency, so each mixed mode sits at its own whole-number value of that phase, and solves for every mode with Newton's method (with a bisection safeguard). Each mixed mode keeps its number as the star evolves, which is what connects it into one line through time. As q → 0 the mixed modes return to the pure p and g modes.
Échelle diagrams
In the frequency échelle the pure p modes line up at ν mod Δν = εp Δν; in the period échelle the pure g modes line up at P mod ΔΠ1 = εg ΔΠ1 (dashed lines). Mixed modes bend away from both. Their marker size shows the p character 1 − ζ, where ζ is the fraction of the mode's inertia in the g cavity, so p-like modes are large and g-like modes are small.
