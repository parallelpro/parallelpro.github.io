The evolving oscillations of a Sun-like star

Follow a star of one solar mass from the pre-main sequence, through the main sequence, to the lower red giant branch. Each step of the slider is one model: its place on the H–R diagram, its internal structure, where waves can travel inside it, and its oscillation frequencies.

Drag the slider, press Play, or use the ← → keys. Click a grey dot on the H–R diagram to jump to that model. Hover any panel for values.

How to read the panels

H–R diagram
The full evolutionary track. Grey dots are the models whose oscillation frequencies were computed; the red marker is the one selected.
Temperature–density structure
Temperature against density through the star, from the centre (upper right) to the surface (lower left). The dashed lines mark where radiation pressure equals gas pressure and where electrons become degenerate, drawn for a fixed solar-like composition as a guide.
Propagation diagram
Pressure (p) modes travel where the frequency is above both N and S1; gravity (g) modes travel where it is below both. On the main sequence N stays well below νmax. After core hydrogen is exhausted the core contracts, N rises above νmax, and dipole modes near νmax take on both p and g character: mixed modes. Gaps in N are convective zones.
Échelle diagram
Frequencies cut into slices of the large separation Δν and stacked, so modes of each degree ℓ line up in near-vertical ridges. For ℓ = 1 and 2 the marker size follows the mode inertia relative to radial modes at the same frequency: full-size markers are p-like, small dots are g-dominated mixed modes. Δν and ε come from a straight-line fit to the radial modes, weighted toward νmax.

Model: 1.0 solar mass, X = 0.7196, Z = 0.0165, mixing length α = 1.71, evolved with YREC. Adiabatic frequencies computed with GYRE. Curves are simplified to screen resolution for the web.
