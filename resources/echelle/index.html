Stretched échelle diagrams

Fold a power spectrum, a list of mode frequencies, or both into three échelle diagrams at once: the usual frequency échelle, the stretched period échelle that lines up dipole mixed modes by their g-mode period spacing, and the stretched frequency échelle that recovers the pure p modes. Tune the asymptotic parameters and watch the ridges straighten.

How it works

Stretched period
Each frequency is mapped to a period τ that removes the p-mode modulation of dipole mixed modes (Mosser et al. 2015). With the right ΔΠ1, q and p-mode phases, the g-dominated modes fall on vertical ridges when τ is folded at ΔΠ1.
τ = 1/ν + (ΔΠ1/π) arctan[ q / tan π(ν/Δν − ½ − εp − d01) ]
Stretched frequency
The mirror transformation removes the g-mode modulation instead, so the dipole p modes line up at ν mod Δν ≈ (εp + ½ + d01) Δν. See the TASC8 talk on decoupling mixed modes.
f = ν − (Δν/π) arctan[ q / tan π(εg − 1/(ν ΔΠ1)) ]
Your own data
A power spectrum is a text or CSV file with a frequency column in μHz and a power column. Column names such as freq and power or snr are recognised; otherwise the first two columns are used. A background-normalised spectrum (S/B) works best. Mode lists need a frequency column and optionally a degree column l; without one every mode is treated as ℓ = 1. After loading, set νmax and Δν for your star.
Rows and axes
As in clickelle, each row of an échelle is one stack of the fold, and a mode is drawn in the row whose stack contains it. The vertical axis is labelled with the median frequency of each row, so it stays truthful even where the stretched period échelle has rows of unequal width.

The fold and stretching follow the clickelle code. The two example spectra and their mode identifications come from clickelle's examples (MIT licence).
